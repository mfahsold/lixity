# An invented night shift

## The quiet room

Across the river, a row of workshop windows holds the last light of the afternoon, while the water below carries a slow procession of leaves toward the bridge. In the signal house, Mara sets a blue cup beside the ledger and opens the narrow window above the radiator. The room smells of warm dust, damp wool and the orange she peeled on the train.

A ferry waits at the far landing. Its passengers are small shapes behind the glass, some standing with bags between their feet, others bent over phones that brighten their faces. From this distance, nobody seems to be going anywhere. Even the clock above the door moves without making an audible demand.

Mara smooths the empty page with her palm. Tonight she will record the departures, answer the radio and leave a clean desk for the morning shift. Those are ordinary tasks, and she has done them often enough to know how an uneventful evening should begin. Beyond the window, a gull balances on the railing, its feathers lifted by the first breath of wind.

## The loose lamp

The lamp flashes. Then darkness. Mara stands. Her cup tips. Tea crosses the page. She catches the ledger and lifts it clear of the desk. Outside, the ferry's engine starts. A bell rings once. The bridge lamp flashes again, pale against the gathering clouds.

She takes the radio. No answer. She tries the spare. A click, a hiss, then a voice cut short. The small torch hangs beside the door. She grabs it. The stairs are wet. At the bottom, a loose board shifts beneath her boot. She holds the rail and waits until it settles.

Water strikes the wall. The lamp cable swings beside its bracket. She points the torch upward. The glass is whole. One bolt is missing. A second turns under her fingers. She pulls the cable away from the metal edge, but the wind pushes it back. Her sleeve catches on the bracket. She frees it. The ferry moves. She waves the torch. Once. Twice. Across the water, someone raises an arm. The engine slows.

## The radio conversation

"Have you stopped the ferry?" Ivo asks through the radio. His voice arrives between two bursts of static. Mara has returned to the room, where the spilled tea is drying around a pale square that had been covered by the ledger.

"I signalled from the landing. I couldn't reach anyone on either set. The lamp cable is touching the bracket, and the wind keeps moving it."

"Keep clear of it. I'll call the maintenance crew. Can you see the boat from there?"

"It's beside the old steps. There are people on the upper deck. One of them is holding a red bag. I think they're waiting for an announcement."

"Tell me what you can see, and leave the rest open. Has anybody come ashore?"

"Not yet. A crew member has put out a rope. I can't see the lower deck."

The radio falls quiet. Mara notices how firmly she is gripping it and loosens her hand. "Ivo, the spare set worked on the stairs. It failed here."

"Then use the stairs if you need to. I'll stay on this channel."

## The incident note

At 19:12, an interruption of the bridge signal was recorded from the signal house. Visual inspection from the landing identified movement of the external lamp cable and the absence of one bracket bolt. Contact between the cable and the metal edge was observed during a gust. The condition of the internal connection was not established.

The ferry was signalled to wait beside the old landing steps. Confirmation of receipt was given by a raised arm from the vessel. No passenger disembarkation was observed from the signal house during the following interval. The lower deck remained outside the observer's field of view.

Communication with the duty operator was restored using the spare radio. The maintenance crew was requested. The cause of the earlier radio failure could not be determined during this shift. A connection between the signal interruption and the radio problem should not be assumed without further inspection.

This note records the observations available before the arrival of the maintenance crew. Departure times and subsequent repair work will be entered separately after confirmation by the responsible operator. No conclusion about the condition of the electrical supply is included.

## The dry page

Mara reads the note twice. In the first version, she had written that the wind caused the failure. Now the sentence is gone. The wind moved the cable; she saw that much. What happened inside the lamp is still somebody else's inspection, and the room offers no answer simply because it has become quiet again.

Her father once kept a notebook for every small repair in the house. He wrote down the part he replaced, the tool he borrowed and the day the same noise returned. As a child, she found those pages dull. Tonight she remembers the patience behind them, the space he left between what he had noticed and what he hoped was true.

She tears a clean sheet from the pad and puts it under the cup. The orange peel has curled into a dry ribbon. Downstairs, boots scrape on the landing, followed by a short knock. Mara opens the door for the maintenance crew and hands over the torch. The ledger stays on the desk, open at the unfinished line.

## Morning handover

By morning, the lamp is steady. A replacement bolt shines against the older metal, and a small loop of cable has been fixed clear of the bracket. The repair note lies beside the ledger. It names the work completed and leaves the radio fault for a separate visit. Mara places the two pages together without combining their conclusions.

Her replacement arrives carrying bread in a paper bag. They walk through the departure list, the waiting interval and the calls that need to be returned. At the radio, he asks which set she used. She shows him the spare and describes where it worked. He writes the location on his own pad.

The first ferry of the day draws away from the landing. A woman on the upper deck lifts a scarf into the wind, then folds it around her neck. Mara closes the window, rinses the cup and puts the torch back beside the door. The desk is almost as she found it. Only the pale square in the tea stain marks the place where the ledger stood.
