Evidence is not the same as an author’s decision.

A source records what you retained. A claim records what you assert. A decision records how you choose to use it in the book, including deliberate departures from fact.

Keep those connections in a dossier with source images, maps and notes. Exact linked versions remain available when a record changes; newer entries do not silently replace earlier pins.

Read it aloud. Keep the words and direction together.

Current main, unreleased: in the local app, Project → Settings → Audio and services groups author recordings, voice profiles, microphone controls and service readiness. Listen & direct also opens from the manuscript toolbar. The public review demo cannot record or start a speech service.

  1. Review a reference. Record or import a short WAV, check the actual transcript and confirm it before saving. A compact player provides time, seeking, volume and a frequency spectrum. Browser microphone permission is separate from hardware gain.
  2. Direct a passage. Choose a saved voice, pronunciation substitutions and pauses. Optional IMS-Toucan supports German/English IPA overrides and structured duration/prosody controls; free acting prompts are not supported by that engine. Other engines have their own capabilities.
  3. Preview before a long render. Export direction as JSON or SSML without synthesis, or explicitly use the configured local or external service. The Teleprompter follows the same spoken text and cues; WAV/JSON take exchange preserves reviewed transcript metadata.

A scrolling rate is not speech alignment, and a reading script is not a verified transcript. Lixity does not run speech recognition. Record and review · Set up phonetic author voices · Speech-plan formats and privacy.

Unreleased listening copies: reader exports can opt into existing chapter recordings that match the current manuscript and export cap. The copy uses the shared player and offers WAV downloads; it does not synthesize speech or contain private voice references or service settings. Chapters without a recording remain text-only. Include chapter audio.

The reader demo’s optional chapter-one audio is an imported eSpeak NG 1.51 recording of the fictional English text (voice en, rate 165). It demonstrates playback and export, not IMS-Toucan voice quality or an author’s cloned voice. Playback starts only when you press Play.

Chapter progress is a reading aid, not access control. For an excerpt, an export cap omits later content before the copy is built. Choose what to include.

Your language. Your manuscript.

Seven interface languages: Deutsch · English · Français · Español · Italiano · Português · Nederlands.

Choose the interface language and the manuscript’s analysis language separately. German and English have the broadest analysis resources; coverage and validation differ for the other languages. Read the language coverage (English).

Technical documentation is in English. The public demo uses invented English prose. Interface translation does not translate your manuscript or imply equal analysis coverage.
