# Research & Dossiers 📂

Lixity bridges the gap between factual reference materials and narrative prose.

---

## 1. The Three-Tier Model: Source, Claim, Decision

Lixity keeps historical evidence and literary fiction cleanly separated:

```
[ Retained Source ] ──> [ Factual Claim ] ──> [ Authorial Decision ]
 (What the document says)  (What you assert)     (How the novel departs or adapts)
```

1. **Source:** Immutable reference document (archival maps, photographs, historical records, articles).
2. **Claim:** An asserted factual point linked to cited manuscript quotations.
3. **Decision:** The documented creative choice (e.g. *“Relocating the historic 1966 tidal flood to November to align with the murder timeline”*).

---

## 2. Structuring Dossiers

A **dossier** organizes context, citations, images, and notes around a character, setting, or historical theme.

* **Rich Typography:** Full CommonMark Markdown, LaTeX mathematical formatting (`$...$` and `$$...$$`), and GitHub-style callouts (`[!NOTE]`, `[!TIP]`, `[!WARNING]`).
* **Side-by-Side Reading:** Open a dossier in the companion drawer next to your manuscript to verify realia while revising.
* **Collections:** Categorize dossiers into logical groups (e.g., *Harbor Topography*, *Forensic Science*, *Historical Biographies*).

---

## 3. Grounding & Vocabulary Comparison

The **Grounding** tool compares reference source vocabulary against your manuscript chapters:

* **Jaccard Index & Simpson Overlap Coefficient:** Measure shared lexical types.
* **Keyness Analysis (Dunning $G^2$):** Identifies characteristic period terms prominent in the source.
* **Chapter Density:** Visualizes in which chapters the reference vocabulary is concentrated.
* **Click-to-Search:** Clicking any term immediately reveals all occurrences in the manuscript.
