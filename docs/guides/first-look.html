A three-minute first look

Follow one project from chapter comparison to a passage, its research and a PDF copy. The same manuscript and records appear throughout this short walkthrough.

Used while writing a debut novel

Matthias Fahsold developed Lixity as an architect and developer at ROST Services GmbH. He used it while writing his own debut novel. The example below shows a review workflow with entirely invented text; it contains no passages from that manuscript.

Open the example

The Crate at Warehouse 4 follows Mara through a fictional harbour incident. Its six short English chapters include setting, action, dialogue and an incident note. Analysis and Research are two views of this same project.

Open the demo project

The demo uses all three native workspace views. Explore manuscript analysis and retained research, keep a Dossier alongside the manuscript, download the claim-evidence matrix or choose a prepared PDF layout. Actions marked Local app, including Search and Compare, are disabled previews with an installation link. The demo cannot import, edit or save; no installation, upload or account is needed.

Enable JavaScript for paragraph expansion, view switching, research and charts. Paragraph text, chapter tables, source credits and PDF links remain readable without it.

1. Notice a chapter contrast

The Start your review panel offers Open passage, Explore chapters and, when measurements are available, Open sentence rhythm. The passage link starts with the first tense flag in source order at the selected threshold, or the opening when none does. It reveals and focuses the source text; the choice is a starting point for reading, not a quality ranking.

In Manuscript & Analysis, open the chapter map and compare chapter 1's setting with chapter 2's action. Look at sentence length and variation. The values describe the supplied text; a colour points to a pattern rather than rating the writing.

Choose one contrast that makes you curious. You do not need to inspect every measurement.

2. Open a paragraph and read

Select a paragraph block in the action chapter to open its text and source lines. You can also use Tab to focus a block, then press Enter or Space. Read the paragraph alongside the opening description.

The passage appears first; expand Values when you need its measurements. Select a chapter in the style dimensions to inspect its scores and return to its passage; nearby help explains how to read the view. Keep the scene's purpose in mind before treating a difference as something to change.

Ask what changed: the kind of scene, what the character is doing, or how the sentences are arranged. Measurements cannot answer that question without the passage.

3. Follow the passage to its research

Switch to Research and open Warehouse 4 case notes. Inspect its historical map and harbour view, then open the linked Attempted warehouse entry claim and retained customs-log passage.

In its geography view, select a place or route to read its context and frame it on the map. The view explains its coordinates, distances and limits.

Open Review to find the saved quote: “Someone had tried the eastern gate of Warehouse 4.” The source passage, authored claim and manuscript quotation are separate records connected by an explicit author association. The log, people and incident are invented; the credited historical images do not verify them.

A brisk action passage and an incident note can serve different purposes. There is no quality score to maximize, and a retained source does not establish factual truth. Read the interpretation guide before acting on a signal.

4. Compare copies of the same manuscript

Open the prepared editorial copy, reading copy, submission sheets or analysis report. All four use these same six chapters. The PDF guide explains their purposes and limits.

Use Lixity with your own project

To analyze your own manuscript or keep research notes and decisions, install Lixity on your computer and open the local workspace. The public demo is read-only; the local workspace supplies the editing and capture actions.
