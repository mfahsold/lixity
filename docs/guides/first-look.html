A three-minute first look

Open one marked sentence. See which words counted, read the sentence in its scene, and decide whether to keep it as it is or revisit it.

Kurze Einführung auf Deutsch

Updated 26 November 2026

This walkthrough follows the generated demo. It uses the same reading website export available in Project actions → Reading website. Its manuscript, analysis and chosen Research are a fixed snapshot; importing and saving require a local project. See how to make your own reading website.

1. Check a marked sentence

Open the demo project

  1. Open Try the draft examples and choose Check a sticky sentence to jump to the marked sentence.
  2. For that paragraph, choose … → Paragraph details → Explain this passage. Under Why this is marked, see the 22 function words that Lixity counted among 31 words. Read the surrounding paragraph and check whether the connections are easy to follow.
  3. Press Escape or close the explanation to continue reading. In your draft, keep the wording if it fits the scene, or revise it and compare the versions using the same settings.

Use the browser exercise to edit a sample paragraph and see its simplified counts change.

2. Compare the rhythm of two scenes

The Crate at Warehouse 4 has six invented English chapters. In Try the draft examples, choose Inspect chapter 2, then read the action scene. Compare chapters 1 and 2 opens the sentence-length overview.

A board moved. Mara turned. The crate tipped towards the drain.
  1. Read the first paragraph of The Loose Board. What is Mara doing, and where do you pause?
  2. Choose its … → Paragraph details → Explain this passage. The card shows the selected sentence. “A board moved.” counts as three words. Read Why this is marked and What to check while reading.
  3. Read chapter 1's harbour description using the opening chapter link in the task card. Compare the reading experience, not just the averages.

A possible decision: keep the short beats because they separate physical actions. Or, if the scene sounds mechanically chopped when read aloud, try joining an action to its consequence in your own draft. The measurement explains the difference; it cannot decide whether the rhythm works. These short synthetic chapters are not a calibration corpus.

You have completed the first check when you can explain the contrast in the text. Open Try the draft examples in the demo to find a wordy sentence, a repeated phrase across three speakers and a shift from past to present. Use the same workflow with your manuscript.

More controls, marks and chapter comparisons

Turn on Analysis and choose what to inspect. Text matches opens counted words or sentences. Under Reading marks, choose Underline counted passages for marks in the prose or Mark differing paragraphs for comparisons within a chapter. Escape closes the evidence card. How it’s measured opens the full method.

For a full-manuscript view, open Analysis overview. In the development demo, it starts at Manuscript measurements. Choose Review passages, Compare chapters or Explore patterns to go deeper. Back to passage restores your reading position.

In Sentence rhythm, choose a length group to read a counted sentence. In Motifs & repetition, open a recurring phrase to move between its occurrences. The reading toolbar takes you through the examples. Read the mark key or see the first-session steps.

3. Follow a quotation into research

Someone had tried the eastern gate of Warehouse 4.

In the read-only demo: this opening quotation has an explicitly saved association. Follow it from the manuscript:

  1. At the opening paragraph, choose … → Paragraph details → Research for this passage. The right context shows its linked claim and dossier while the text stays visible.
  2. Open a title to read the current entry and its sources, or choose Linked version for the exact earlier version used by the association. Versions expands its history without changing the entry. A retained source and an authored claim are separate records; the association does not establish factual truth.
  3. Close the viewer and use Back to passage to resume reading. On narrow screens the context panel sits above the text; its close button or Escape returns you to the paragraph.
Explore dossiers and review

In Research → Dossiers, open Warehouse 4 case notes. Its Sections list follows the actual headings; close and reopen it as needed. Choose Dossier alongside to keep the dossier beside the manuscript.

From an open dossier, Back to dossier restores its saved version and reading position after you follow a connection. Review also lists the saved quotation; Go to manuscript passage follows its exact text match. Reader guidance covers controls and keyboard shortcuts.

In Research → Claims, open the timeline to compare the dated records. The watchman’s report, the dispatch and the corrected inspection belong to different moments. The visit with an unknown date stays separate.

People, events and journeys are fictional. The historical images do not verify them. Maps in this website use local sketches and geographic shapes without requesting online map tiles. Sources and PDF copies explains retention and saving in the local app.

Read with selected analysis

Open the synthetic reading copy, choose Analysis above the story, and inspect its chapter overview. Choose Back to reading copy to return to the story and its selected background. This example deliberately includes chapter figures for all six chapters; reading progress does not hide analysis. In your own export, select only the analysis you want to share and use a chapter cap to omit later content.

Choose the reader’s route

  • Review workspace: read the manuscript with its analysis and included research records.
  • Novel with companion notes: read continuous prose, confirm chapter progress and open the selected background. The Analysis link opens the measurements deliberately included in this copy.
  • Standalone companion: explore those selected notes while reading the novel elsewhere.

A reading copy is fixed. Reading progress hides or reveals included notes, though the underlying files remain in the export. In your own export, use a chapter cap to omit later material. Since 0.8.1: --include-audio adds already recorded chapter WAVs matching the current manuscript, with a shared player and download. It does not start a speech worker or include private voice references. Audio inclusion and limits.

Open Chapter audio to hear the first two chapters in a synthetic English preset voice, or download their WAV files. In the author workspace, open Research → Dossiers → Warehouse 4 exhibit book to play the watchman’s captioned statement and read its transcript. The reader and companion reveal the statement after chapter three. The dispatch receipt and ledger appear later in the full reading copy.

4. Choose a PDF copy

Use the direct PDF links below or in the demo page; the exported website does not generate new PDFs. Compare the same manuscript as an editorial copy, reading copy, submission sheets or analysis report. The PDF guide explains their purposes and limits.

Use your own project

Manuscript input supports Markdown (.md, .markdown) and plain text (.txt). The file importer also accepts one Word/DOCX file. Export Scrivener documents to a supported file format first; inspect the import preview before creating a project. PDFs can be retained separately as Research sources.

Install the local app and launch the workspace. Choose Open Project to continue with an existing folder. Choose Analyze a manuscript to review a copy before creating a new project. Confirm the analysis language. See running the local service if the browser reopens an older workspace.

Hear a passage in your own project

Use Teleprompter (P) to rehearse a passage in a local project. The public demo plays existing chapter recordings. Use the local app to capture your own voice, save a reference or generate speech.

  1. Open Project → Settings → Audio and services → Record author's voice. Allow microphone access, read a short passage and listen to the take. Check the words you actually spoke before you choose Save as author voice for this book. The recording guide covers microphone permission and imported takes.
  2. Open Voice profiles. If the take is not active, choose a profile that supports a Reference recording, select the take and save the profile. Check the engine setup under Speech service. Then use Listen and direct → Preview paragraph in the manuscript to hear a sample. Choose a synthesis route
  3. Use Export direction → Preview export to review or download the planned delivery as JSON or SSML. This works offline without the synthesis service. Once you have listened to the paragraph preview, use Chapter audio → Render chapter.

For German or English with IPA input and structured prosody, use a saved reference with IMS-Toucan. In v0.9.3, save pronunciations in the voice profile. The development checkout adds a project pronunciation dictionary with IPA and alternate spellings.

In the local app, open Project for a read-only overview of the selected manuscript, research folder and existing outputs. It inspects metadata without rebuilding analysis; where an output came from and how it was generated remain unknown. The exported demo has no local project-folder view. Project inspection and CLI.
