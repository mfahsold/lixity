A three-minute first look

Read one manuscript, inspect a passage, follow its research and choose a PDF copy. The same manuscript and records appear throughout this short walkthrough.

Used while writing a debut novel

Matthias Fahsold developed Lixity as an architect and developer at ROST Services GmbH. He used it while writing his own debut novel. The example below shows a review workflow with entirely invented text; it contains no passages from that manuscript.

Open the example

The Crate at Warehouse 4 follows Mara through a fictional harbour incident. Its six short English chapters include setting, action, dialogue and an incident note. Analysis and Research are two views of this same project.

Open the demo project

The demo uses all three native workspace views. Explore manuscript analysis and retained research, keep a Dossier alongside the manuscript, download the claim-evidence matrix or choose a prepared PDF layout. Actions marked Local app, including Search and Compare, are disabled previews with an installation link. The demo cannot import, edit or save; no installation, upload or account is needed.

Enable JavaScript for the Analysis display and overview controls, context tools, view switching, research and charts. Paragraph text, chapter tables, source credits and PDF links remain readable without it. The demo follows current development; an installed release may have an earlier interface.

1. Read a chapter

In Manuscript, the manuscript initially presents the chapters as continuous prose. Scroll normally, or choose a chapter in the reader toolbar. Read chapter 1's setting and chapter 2's action before looking at their numbers.

Use A−/A+ to adjust text size. Reading settings offers text width and line spacing; the same heading closes it. These display choices stay in this browser and do not change the manuscript. Focus reading temporarily clears navigation and side tools; choose Leave focus or press Escape to return.

Reading uses ordinary text selection. Maps, images and style-space keep their own pan and zoom controls; the manuscript remains a scrollable document.

2. Inspect a passage when needed

Open a paragraph's … button for its tools. Right-clicking the prose opens the same menu; with the paragraph button focused, Shift+F10 works too. Arrow keys move between actions and Escape closes the menu. Values opens a paragraph inspector with source lines and measurements; its close button or Escape returns you to the passage. Local marker and claim actions appear when their capabilities are available; selecting text within one paragraph also offers a small toolbar.

Choose Analysis overview for chapter comparisons and expandable paragraph blocks. The Start your review panel offers Open passage, Explore chapters and, when measurable, Open sentence rhythm. A passage link starts with the first tense flag in source order at the selected threshold, or the opening when none does. This is a starting point for reading, not a quality ranking.

Compare the opening and action chapter's sentence length and variation. Use Analysis overview for comparisons and Back to passage to resume reading. On a paragraph, open … → Values for its measurements. Analysis shows a visible On/Off state and leaves the manuscript text and anchors unchanged. Choose one contrast that makes you curious; you do not need to inspect every measurement.

Current development: the wide-screen demo keeps the project and workspace views in a fixed sidebar. On mobile, the views sit above the text. Manuscript and analysis come first; a dossier can accompany the passage when you need research context. Opening it collapses the desktop sidebar to make room; use Show navigation to reopen it. Closing the dossier restores your previous choice. Mobile navigation stays visible.

With a dossier alongside on desktop, drag the divider to give either document more room. You can also focus it with Tab and use Left/Right; Home/End reach the split limits. Narrow screens stack the documents. Analysis and Dossier are alternative context tools. Switching the Analysis display off closes its measurements but keeps an active dossier. The overview offers a direct return and restores the context tool. Display changes keep the visible paragraph in place where possible.

Ask what changed: the kind of scene, what the character is doing, or how the sentences are arranged. Measurements cannot answer that question without the passage.

3. Follow the passage to its research

Switch to Research and open Warehouse 4 case notes. Inspect its historical map and harbour view, then open the linked Attempted warehouse entry claim and retained customs-log passage.

In its geography view, select a place or route to read its context and frame it on the map. The view explains its coordinates, distances and limits.

Open Review to find the saved quote: “Someone had tried the eastern gate of Warehouse 4.” The source passage, authored claim and manuscript quotation are separate records connected by an explicit author association. The log, people and incident are invented; the credited historical images do not verify them.

Saved exact associations offer Go to manuscript passage and Open claim. The passage action returns to matching text without rewriting it. Changed or ambiguous quotations require review before an exact return is offered. In the local workspace, select a quotation to connect it to an existing claim, then review retention and save explicitly.

Research creation forms start collapsed. Select a heading to open it and select it again to collapse it; long forms also offer Collapse beside Save. Unsaved fields remain across tab switches and collapse, but reloads are not draft storage. Escape closes the innermost disclosure and returns focus to its heading. Dossier previews show readable text; open the full dossier for formatting, diagrams and provenance. Brief disclosure motion respects reduced-motion preferences.

A brisk action passage and an incident note can serve different purposes. There is no quality score to maximize, and a retained source does not establish factual truth. Read the interpretation guide before acting on a signal.

4. Compare copies of the same manuscript

Open the prepared editorial copy, reading copy, submission sheets or analysis report. All four use these same six chapters. The PDF guide explains their purposes and limits.

Use Lixity with your own project

To analyze your own manuscript or keep research notes and decisions, install Lixity on your computer and open the local workspace. The public demo is read-only; the local workspace supplies the editing and capture actions.
