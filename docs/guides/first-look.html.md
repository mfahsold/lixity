# A three-minute first look

Read one manuscript, inspect a passage, follow its research and choose a PDF copy. The same manuscript and records appear throughout this short walkthrough.

Maintained by the Lixity project · Updated 21 October 2026

## 1. Open the example and read a chapter

[Open the demo project](../demo/report.html)

*The Crate at Warehouse 4* has six invented English chapters, one research archive and four prepared PDF layouts. Read chapter 1's setting and [chapter 2's action](../demo/report.html#ch-2). No installation, account or upload is needed. The read-only demo cannot import, edit or save; actions marked **Local app** require [installation](installation.html).

## 2. Inspect one difference

Choose a paragraph's **… → Values** to open its **Passage values** inspector. The separate **Analysis** switch shows diagnostics across the manuscript; Passage values remains usable with Analysis On or Off. Use **Analysis overview** to compare sentence rhythm in the setting and action chapters, then **Back to passage** to resume reading.

**On current main (Unreleased):** expand **Manuscript measurements** for the secondary numbers, then collapse the same summary to return to the compact overview. A linked scene title reveals its detailed **Scene measurements**; both disclosure levels can be closed again. The passage return keeps your earlier reading context.

Ask what changed: the kind of scene, what the character is doing, or how the sentences are arranged. These short chapters demonstrate a workflow; their numbers are not a quality verdict. [Interpretation](interpretation.html) explains the signals. [Reader controls and coverage](../USAGE.md#reading-paragraphs) covers text size, focus, keyboard access and supported formatting.

## 3. Follow a quotation into research

Someone had tried the eastern gate of Warehouse 4.

**In the development demo:** this opening quotation has an explicitly saved association. Follow it from the manuscript:

1. At the opening paragraph, choose **… → Saved research**. The right context shows the saved claim and dossier versions while the text stays visible.

2. Open the claim title to read that version and its retained sources. A retained source and an authored claim are separate records; the association does not establish factual truth.

3. Close the viewer and use **Back to passage** to resume reading. On narrow screens the context sits above the text; its close button or Escape returns to the paragraph.

**Explore dossiers and review**

In [**Research → Dossiers**](../demo/report.html#research-manager), open **Warehouse 4 case notes**. Its **Sections** list follows the actual headings; close and reopen it as needed. Choose **Dossier alongside** to keep the dossier beside the manuscript.

From an open dossier, **Back to dossier** restores its saved version and reading position after you follow a connection. **Review** also lists the saved quotation; **Go to manuscript passage** follows its exact text match. [Reader guidance](../USAGE.md#reading-paragraphs) covers controls and keyboard shortcuts.

People, events and journeys are fictional. The historical images do not verify them. Maps use OpenStreetMap and send the viewed area and client IP to its tile provider; turn off the background or choose the sketch for offline use. [Sources and PDF copies](research-pdf.html) explains retention and saving in the local app.

## Use your own project

[Install the local app](installation.html), then open your project or preview a manuscript import. Analysis runs on your computer. JavaScript enables the demo's interactive controls; manuscript text, tables, credits and PDF links remain readable without it.
