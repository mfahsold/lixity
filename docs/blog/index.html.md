# Notes on measurable manuscript revision

Practical articles for reading a draft, checking its research and investigating a question: is this change of voice deliberate, is a phrase repeating too close by, or did the chapter's tense drift? Each article connects a tool to a reading decision. All examples use public-domain or synthetic texts.

## [Give the manuscript room to read](manuscript-reading-workbench.html)

Updated 21 October 2026 · 6 minutes

Inspect the text behind a measurement, follow a passage's saved claim and source, then return to the sentence. Try the complete loop in the invented Warehouse 4 manuscript.

## [When a chapter is meant to sound different](intentional-registers.html)

Published 19 October 2026 · 4 minutes

A letter, a child's account or a formal record can differ on purpose. Record that intention with a named chapter register, add optional author ranges and understand which flags the assignment sets aside.

## [Finding repeated phrases close together](local-word-echoes.html)

Published 19 October 2026 · 4 minutes

Repetition across a book and repetition inside one passage raise different questions. Count a synthetic example yourself, see how local windows and overlapping phrases affect the result, then read the echo in context.

## [What's new in Lixity: numbers you can compare, evidence that survives edits](whats-new-lixity.html)

Posted 16 October 2026 · 7 minutes

Documented comparability floors replace big-looking statistics from short chapters, evidence anchors now tolerate edits outside the quote (with an explicit re-pin), and the interface, research workspace and service logs are consolidated. What changed in Lixity 0.4.8.

## [Finding tense switches in a novel — and deciding which ones matter](tense-switches.html)

Posted 15 October 2026 · 6 minutes

Readers notice tense breaks long before they can name them. This workflow article shows how paragraph-level profiles and a flagged-passage list turn that vague feeling into passages you can review one by one — and when a switch is deliberate.

## [A reference corridor from *Effi Briest*: measuring a draft against its own model](effi-briest-case-study.html)

Posted 15 October 2026 · 7 minutes

A walkthrough of the public example analysis: how a self-calibrating reference (robust median/MAD, noise-adjusted deviations, FDR selection) describes *where* a manuscript changes — without turning measurements into verdicts.
