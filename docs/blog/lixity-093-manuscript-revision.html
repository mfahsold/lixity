Who sees the scratches first?

By Matthias Fahsold · Published 26 November 2026 · 4 minutes

Here is the first paragraph of the Warehouse 4 draft:

Mara stopped beneath the quay lamp. Someone had tried the eastern gate of Warehouse 4. Fresh scratches crossed the lock, and an abandoned crate stood beside the wall.

Swap the last two sentences. Now you see the scratches and the crate while you are still wondering what happened at the gate. The opening has 28 words and three sentences either way. I would try the scratches first, then read into the next paragraph and see whether the crate draws the eye too soon. Read the sample opening

Try the swap in a draft

In a local project, choose Write → Versions → Compare versions. Try the swap in Draft to review and read it beside the saved file. Lixity 0.9.3 uses the same analysis settings for both. If you bring the candidate into the editor, Lixity asks before replacing different unsaved text. Built-in analysis runs locally on your computer. Version comparison

For a clue that arrives several scenes later, open Story rehearsal. Write “Who tried the gate?” on a Question card and give the clue its own Revelation card. Move the cards to see how long you would leave the question open. The manuscript and its measurements stay in their original order while you try it. Story rehearsal

Hear where the clue lands

Read both openings aloud. In the revised order I would leave a pause after the crate, just before we learn someone tried the gate. Listen and direct keeps the paragraph in view while you write that direction down. Author’s voice lets you review a microphone take before keeping it.
