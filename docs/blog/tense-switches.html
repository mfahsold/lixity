Finding tense switches in a novel — and deciding which ones matter

Posted 15 October 2026 · 6 minutes · Blog

BlogFirst lookInterpret resultsExample analysis

Why tense breaks are a macro-editing topic

A tense break is rarely a grammar mistake. In a novel, the base tense is a narrative decision: past for a retrospective narrator, present for immediate scenes, historical present for pressure. What readers notice is not a rule being broken but a signal being sent — often at the wrong moment. Reading an entire manuscript for those signals is slow; that is why editors work with passage-level evidence instead of impressions.

What Lixity measures

Lixity profiles tense at two levels. Every paragraph gets a heuristic dominance — present, past, mixed or neutral — with a severity score from 0 to 3 for paragraphs that switch tense or mix it noticeably. Every chapter gets a dominant tense as well, which is what readers experience as the “base” of a scene.

Two result sets matter here, and they are different things:

  • Flagged paragraphs (severity ≥ 2) are the short list for review: passages where a switch or a strong mix shows up inside one paragraph.
  • Chapter dominance describes the surrounding norm. Without it, a switch has no reference point.

The profiles are heuristics over verb forms and markers, not a grammatical parse. Dialogue, embedded clauses and reported speech all shape the counts — which is exactly why the workflow ends with a human reading the passage, not with a number.

The workflow, step by step

  1. Install and open. uv tool install lixity, then lixity service start --open and choose Open Project for your manuscript folder. (See the installation guide for Windows, macOS and Linux.)
  2. Start with the review panel. Start your review offers Open passage (the first paragraph that meets your flag threshold, in source order), Explore chapters and Open sentence rhythm.
  3. Work the flagged list. The Flagged passages panel lists every paragraph at or above the threshold, sorted by severity and line. Each row jumps into the manuscript: the paragraph opens, the page scrolls, the passage flashes — with the “flagged only” filter set, so the rest of the page disappears.
  4. Read the context before judging. Expand Values inside the opened paragraph for the source lines and measurements. Check the scene: is the switch carried by a letter, a memory, an interior monologue? The chapter line in the header shows the chapter’s dominant tense, so you can see whether the paragraph deviates from its surroundings.
  5. Record the decision where you read it. The marker buttons (+ To-do, + Check, + Attention) write a review marker into the manuscript as a Markdown comment — invisible in your normal reading view, listed later in the Work markers panel.

When a switch is deliberate

Severity measures a change, not a mistake. Common intentional cases:

  • Letters and documents. A quoted letter often shifts to present or to a different register; readers expect the frame to change.
  • Flashbacks and frame narratives. A chapter that moves from present to past may be doing exactly what the structure asked for.
  • Free indirect speech. A character’s inner voice can pull the tense without a scene break — deliberate, but easy to overuse.
  • Cliffhangers and scene turns. A single present-tense paragraph at the end of a past-tense chapter can be a strong, intentional accent.

The flagged list does not decide any of this. It makes sure that no unintended break hides in a 400-page manuscript, and that intended ones are visible enough to be a choice.

Reading limits

Verb-form heuristics miss irony, ellipsis and unusual syntax; short chapters produce noisy estimates; per-paragraph profiles in dialogue-heavy scenes reflect speaker turns. The interpretation guide and the methods document explain the estimators; the flag_min_severity setting (1–3) controls how strict the flagged list is. Statistical signals support a human review — they are not an instruction to rewrite prose, and they are not a quality score.

Try it without installing

The Effi Briest example and the Pride and Prejudice example are complete, public-domain analyses you can open in the browser. For your own text: install, open the project, and start with a single chapter — the review loop is the same at any length.
